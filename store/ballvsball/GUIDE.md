# Ball vs Ball

An original browser adaptation inspired by [Ball VS Ball on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/96510596525082/Ball-VS-Ball). The reference advertises three random choices each round, auto-battles in 1v1/2v2 and a free collection pick once per match. This adaptation recreates that loop with original art, ability tuning, physics and progression. It does not connect to Roblox or provide online matchmaking.

Choose 1v1 against AI, 2v2 with an AI ally, 4v4 Chaos with eight balls, or a local two-player duel. Each team begins with three hearts. Pick one of three random balls from your collection, aim by dragging anywhere in the arena, and press Lock aim. Combat then runs automatically. Arrows or A/D rotate the aim; Enter locks it. Both local players choose and aim in turn on the same device. The physics, wall bounces and each ball's abilities decide the round. A defeated side loses a heart; a tied round removes a heart from both sides. Losing three hearts ends the match.

Once per match, choose any owned ball instead of the random offer. The first player also has one free offer reroll per match. After thirty seconds, the walls close in and all surviving balls take rising sudden-death damage; at sixty seconds, remaining proportional team health breaks the tie. Pause freezes all physics and cooldowns. Hidden browser tabs pause. A 2× button speeds the simulation without changing physics.

The roster has 60 balls with their own abilities: collision modifiers, fire, frost, poison, life steal, shields, lasers, chain lightning, explosions, growth, healing, phase windows, fragments, magnets, gravity, rockets, wall bonuses, armour, reflectors, satellites, wall chains, frost waves, a one-time revival, delayed meteors, cleansing water, wind, slime, saw teeth, shockwaves, tremors, velocity swapping, homing stingers, needles, crystal volleys, solar aura, lunar drain, elemental cycling, void pools and royal volleys. Rarity is collection rarity; it does not guarantee a win.

Twelve starter balls are free. A match win awards 140 coins plus 25 for each round won; a loss awards 75 plus round-win coins. Direct unlocks cost 300/650/1100 coins for Uncommon/Rare/Legendary balls. Capsules cost 150 coins, with Common 46%, Uncommon 30%, Rare 18%, Legendary 6%. Duplicates refund 60 coins. After four consecutive duplicates, the next capsule guarantees an unowned ball when one remains. There are no purchases with real money.

Settings contain AI difficulty, six arenas or Random, sound, volume and motion effects. Local players always use equal stats. Collection, coins, preferences and statistics save on this browser; an unfinished match is not restored. Uninstalling preserves progress. PocketVM's profile erase clears it.

The supplied `echoes_of_lumen-pixel-art-game-soundtrack-chiptune-588667.mp3` is packaged as `menu.mp3` and plays on the menu. The supplied `tatamusic-chiptune-video-game-games-music-552833.mp3` is packaged as `battle.mp3` and plays during matches. Browsers may require the first click before music starts. Music is muted separately from original collision sounds.

The arena uses a fixed orthographic Canvas view. All combat objects share the simulation's coordinates and bounds; there is no movable camera or 3D near-plane clipping. Touch and mouse use pointer capture for aiming, with cancellation on blur, resizing and hidden tabs. Menus scroll independently on small screens. The battle view contains hearts, a timer, pause/music and speed controls; collection and settings stay behind panels.

The court now adapts its physical width to the fullscreen viewport. Wall and bumper rebounds vary by up to 12 degrees with bounded speed variation; glancing contacts vary slightly while preserving pair momentum and kinetic energy. Match seeds keep runs reproducible. Eighteen additional abilities include bubble shields, dice rolls, portal blinks, spores, clockwork, team healing and delayed echo waves. Bumper positions vary safely between matches.

The fullscreen court uses 88% of the game viewport width and grows vertically up to 88% of its height, with extra space reserved where compact screens need it. Its physical width follows the screen shape, including after an orientation change; balls, bumpers, projectiles and ability zones share those bounds. Spectators stay in their own margins.
